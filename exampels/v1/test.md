## Problem+TA

### Basic

**Project Name**: GrantFlow Studio
**Hypothesis**: If independent artists and small creative teams receive a tool that centralizes grant discovery, matches them to relevant opportunities, helps adapt ideas to requirements, and auto-generates submission-ready documentation, their success rate and application throughput will significantly increase.

---

### B2C

**Geography**: Europe, UK, US, Canada; later — LATAM & East Asia (regions with active public/private arts funding).
**Sex**: All
**Age**: 20–55
**Occupation**: Visual artists, performers, media artists, designers, illustrators, new-media creatives, cultural workers.
**Education**: Art schools, design degrees, or self-taught artists with portfolio experience.
**Lifestyle**: Freelance or hybrid employment; often juggling multiple projects; limited administrative bandwidth; high creative motivation but low tolerance for bureaucratic tasks; digital-native or digital-comfortable.
**Context**: They encounter the problem when searching for grants manually, trying to interpret eligibility criteria, customizing project ideas for different applications, or preparing large volumes of documentation under deadlines.

---

### B2B

**Geography**: EU cultural organizations, UK arts councils, US/Canadian arts nonprofits, foundations, artist-run centers.
**Sector**: Arts institutions, residency programs, creative incubators, museums with emerging-artist programs, grant-funding organizations, universities’ art departments.
**Size**: SMB and Mid-market; some Enterprise-scale foundations.
**Decision Maker**: Program directors, grant managers, heads of artist development, innovation leads.
**Urgency Trigger**: High inbound application volume, need to support artists with better guidance, requirement to increase diversity/inclusion in applicants, seasonal grant cycles.
**Economic Factors**: Pressure to reduce staff time spent on consulting applicants; desire to improve application quality; budget constraints leading to interest in cost-efficient automation.

---

### Problem

**What**:
Artists struggle to find relevant grants, understand nuanced requirements, adapt their ideas to each program, and assemble strong applications. The process is fragmented, time-consuming, and cognitively heavy. Many opportunities are missed due to poor visibility, weak alignment, or documentation fatigue.

**When**:
Occurs during research phases (monthly/seasonally), during project development, and especially during deadline-driven application windows. Peaks around major annual grant cycles.

**How to measure**:

* Time spent per application (baseline vs. reduced)
* Number of relevant opportunities discovered per month
* Drop-off rate between intention to apply and final submission
* Application success rate (self-reported or partner-reported)
* User sentiment around clarity, confidence, and workload

---

### Solution

**Solution**:
A front-end application that lets artists input their background, portfolio, and project ideas. The system generates LLM-structured research packages: tailored grant lists, requirement interpretation, idea-to-grant alignment suggestions, and automatically drafted application materials (concept notes, budgets, artist statements). Artists simply paste prompts into their LLM to generate structured data and return it to the application for visualization. The tool becomes a personal “grant strategist” that reduces administrative load and boosts application readiness.
